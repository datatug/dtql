The immediate freeze blockers are **A-01, A-02, A-03, A-04 and A-06**. A-05 is an implementation-design and acceptance issue that can remain outside the portable wire freeze.

The following are human choices, rather than defects:

- **Retaining DALgo hierarchy and conservative field intersections:** appropriate for compatibility. Explicit field allow-lists, including their treatment of newly added fields, should remain a visible product decision.
- **Per-owner admission snapshots:** coherent, provided approval accepts that already-admitted operations can finish after a policy edit. The packet correctly avoids claiming simultaneous cross-layer revocation.
- **Readable remote evidence plus CAS:** a defensible MVP boundary. Conditional write-only access requiring hidden downstream evidence remains unsupported; approval should acknowledge that limitation.
- **Public-by-default policy classification:** the packet consistently records the user direction and distinguishes authenticated disclosure from anonymous publication. Private-policy administration remains independently owner-scoped.
- **Batch and execution limits:** choose the narrow duplicate-target rejection and concrete bounds unless repeated-key batches are an explicit MVP requirement.
- **Crash recovery:** decide whether policy-store restart must recover automatically to a complete generation or may fail closed pending repair. The current acceptance language promises the former.

Three aspects are explicitly worth preserving:

1. **The ownership and reuse model.** DTQL owns portable contracts; DALgo supplies one reusable evaluator; each owner retains mandatory enforcement and administration. Keeping InGitDB behind its secured adapter preserves the removed-server decision and avoids duplicating authentication infrastructure.
2. **The distinction between assessment and authority.** `conditional` is separate from final allow; lower inspection continues after upper static denial without executing a mutation; private evidence stays within authorized enforcement paths; sample results remain bounded and nonexhaustive.
3. **The real vertical acceptance slice.** Persisted owner policies, browser editing, actual queries and updates, independent direct-lower tests, typed blockers and Git-state assertions provide meaningful evidence. The small structured record editor avoids turning the read-only builder into a SQL mutation system.

Across the remaining rubric dimensions, the design is sound in direction. It correctly separates actor capability from subject policy authority, prevents owner-token data bypass, preserves independent lower-owner administration, moves hidden-field reference enforcement into DALgo, and treats unavailable/custom providers as explicit incomplete enforcement. The DataTug UX handles stale revisions, conditional results, private restrictions and save conflicts without making the browser authoritative. The custom-provider and future change-set notes remain appropriately bounded.

The work packages are substantial—especially shared enforcement, secured adapter forwarding, policy publication and the remote evidence seam—but they identify the right dependencies. Finishing the listed contract fixes before parallel downstream work is more valuable than adding another framework or expanding the supported query/write language.
