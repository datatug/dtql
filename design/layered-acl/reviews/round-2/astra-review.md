The remaining human choice is principally A2-01: how much existing callback-style transaction behavior the protected MVP must support. A bounded supported subset is consistent with the approved narrow profile. Full compatibility is also possible in principle, but requires an explicit short-transaction adaptation design and corresponding effort; it should not emerge accidentally inside WP4b. A2-02 is a correctness repair rather than a scope choice. Selection can be denied without producing a row, so its diagnostics need a representable identity and reducer rule. A2-03 requires clarifying the already-approved privacy promise, rather than reopening public-by-default policies or private admin-only visibility.

Three aspects are particularly worth preserving:

1. **The existing DALgo foundation.** Hierarchical precedence, union of matched bindings within one policy, intersection across policies, and conservative query-field alternatives remain intact. The approved closure correctly avoids treating already-shipped behavior as missing. Keeping protocol conversion in `access/contract` also provides a workable package boundary.

2. **The distinction between private enforcement evidence and remotely readable evidence.** Registered embedded evaluators receive complete images inside the storage boundary; remote DataTug receives only explicitly authorized field coverage plus a whole-record revision. Missing, null and withheld values are distinguished. Together with lower-layer reauthorization and CAS, this addresses the main remote conditional-write risk without introducing distributed transactions.

3. **C7’s restrained execution model.** `User_*` inclusion and `*` with `sys_*` exclusion are representable, exclusions accumulate within a namespace, and independent policy gates intersect. Server-derived classification and unsupported native effects prevent a matching mask from becoming execution authority.
